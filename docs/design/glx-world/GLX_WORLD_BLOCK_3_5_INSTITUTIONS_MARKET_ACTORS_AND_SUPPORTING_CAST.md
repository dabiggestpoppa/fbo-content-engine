# GLX WORLD — BLOCK 3.5: INSTITUTIONS, MARKET ACTORS & SUPPORTING CAST

## Status
Canonical draft for institutional representation, market populations, human supporting roles, rivals, mentors, students, explorers, and audience-proxy characters.

## Purpose
Block 3.5 introduces the social layer of GLX WORLD without collapsing the Market into simplistic villains, faceless conspiracies, or generic “retail vs institutions” mythology.

> **Markets are populated by actors with different incentives, information, capital, constraints, time horizons, and responsibilities—not one hidden team controlling everything.**

This section defines categories and story functions while intentionally delaying permanent names.

---

# 3.5.1 — THE MARKET IS A CIVILIZATION

Market World contains institutions, independent traders, researchers, technicians, capital allocators, policy actors, speculators, hedgers, market makers, long-horizon investors, short-horizon operators, observers, data couriers, and mapmakers.

The world should feel inhabited even when no named character is speaking.

---

# 3.5.2 — ACTORS EXIST FOR REASONS

Every actor needs:
- objective,
- constraint,
- resource base,
- horizon,
- information set,
- risk capacity,
- incentive,
- blind spot.

Two opposite positions can both be rational under different mandates.

---

# 3.5.3 — ACTOR ONTOLOGY

Model institutional/human actors through:

**Objective** — what are they trying to accomplish?  
**Capital** — how much capacity can they deploy?  
**Information** — what can they observe?  
**Horizon** — seconds, hours, months, decades?  
**Constraint** — what are they not allowed to do?  
**Liquidity Need** — how easily must they transact?  
**Risk Capacity** — how much adverse movement can they tolerate?  
**Adaptability** — how quickly can they change?  
**Coordination** — individual, team, or system?  
**Public Visibility** — how visible are their intentions?

---

# 3.5.4 — NO SINGLE “SMART MONEY” CHARACTER

Do not create one character named Smart Money who secretly controls the Market.

Institutional participants disagree constantly because their horizons, mandates, information, and exposures differ.

The institutional layer is an ecosystem.

---

# 3.5.5 — GREAT HOUSES

Large categories of organized capital may be represented as Great Houses:
- Dealer Houses
- Asset Houses
- Sovereign Houses
- Policy Towers
- Systematic Houses
- Hedge Houses
- Commercial Houses

These are categories first. Named factions can emerge later.

---

# 3.5.6 — DEALER HOUSES

Dealer Houses represent institutions facilitating markets and warehousing/transferring risk.

Core motivations:
- liquidity provision,
- inventory management,
- spread capture,
- client flow,
- risk transfer.

Visual language:
- ports,
- exchange halls,
- switching yards,
- warehouses,
- routing infrastructure.

> **Dealer power = access + flow + infrastructure, not omniscient prediction.**

Dealer Houses can misprice risk, become constrained, reduce liquidity, hedge aggressively, and lose money.

---

# 3.5.7 — ASSET HOUSES

Represent long-horizon capital such as asset managers and pensions.

Motivations:
- allocation,
- benchmark management,
- mandate fulfillment,
- long-term return,
- risk budgeting.

Visual:
- huge slow caravans,
- vault-backed convoys,
- long capital roads.

Large mass, slower agility.

---

# 3.5.8 — SOVEREIGN HOUSES

Represent sovereign wealth, government-linked capital, and reserve management.

Visual:
- national-scale vaults,
- protected infrastructure,
- long-range capital fleets,
- diplomatic routes.

Their objectives may include more than pure profit.

---

# 3.5.9 — POLICY TOWERS

Central banks and policy institutions should appear primarily through Policy Towers rather than wizard-like rulers.

Functions:
- rates,
- liquidity facilities,
- reserve systems,
- communication,
- policy signals.

Policy decisions can alter Dollar Gravity, capital routes, Equity conditions, Sanctuary Routes, and borrowing terrain.

They have enormous but conditional influence.

---

# 3.5.10 — SYSTEMATIC HOUSES

Represent algorithms, trend systems, volatility strategies, systematic allocation, and rule-driven execution.

Visual:
- Machine Cities,
- synchronized fleets,
- geometric formations,
- autonomous routes.

Strengths:
- speed,
- consistency,
- rule discipline.

Failure modes:
- regime mismatch,
- shared positioning,
- feedback loops,
- latency,
- flawed data,
- crowding,
- parameter failure.

---

# 3.5.11 — HEDGE HOUSES

Represent highly adaptive tactical capital.

Traits:
- flexible mandates,
- opportunism,
- specialization,
- tactical risk,
- competition.

Smaller and faster than major sovereign/asset infrastructure.

Excellent source pool for future named rivals.

---

# 3.5.12 — COMMERCIAL HOUSES

Represent entities with real economic exposure such as producers, airlines, manufacturers, importers/exporters, and agricultural companies.

They may hedge rather than speculate.

> **Not every market transaction is a directional bet.**

A non-trader sees a company protecting itself; a trader sees hedging flow; an advanced viewer sees real-economy exposure shaping derivative demand.

---

# 3.5.13 — RETAIL POPULATIONS

Retail is diverse:
- beginners,
- discretionary traders,
- systematic independents,
- investors,
- gamblers,
- researchers,
- specialists,
- creators,
- prop traders.

Some are excellent. Some reckless. Some informed. Some noisy.

GLX emerges from the independent human side of the Market.

---

# 3.5.14 — RETAIL CARAVANS

Small, mobile, decentralized participants.

Strengths:
- agility,
- low coordination burden,
- niche focus,
- ability to stay out.

Weaknesses:
- limited capital,
- uneven information,
- emotional vulnerability,
- social contagion.

Retail should never be treated as a uniformly foolish victim class.

---

# 3.5.15 — SOCIAL HERDS

Retail and professionals alike can herd when narratives spread, positioning crowds, social proof dominates, or momentum feeds participation.

Institutions can herd too.

---

# 3.5.16 — INFORMATION BROKERS & HERALDS

Information Brokers represent data vendors, research desks, analysts, news systems, and alternative-data networks.

Heralds transmit information publicly through data, events, rumors, and narratives.

Information can be late, wrong, incomplete, or misinterpreted.

Information quality and interpretation remain separate.

---

# 3.5.17 — MARKET CARTOGRAPHERS

Market Cartographers map relationships, regions, regimes, and historical routes.

GLX is one.

CEREBUS is his advanced technical partner.

Other Cartographers can become allies, rivals, and mentors.

This is an important in-world profession for quants/researchers.

---

# 3.5.18 — RISK WARDENS

Market-side Risk Wardens manage exposure, leverage, limits, and capital preservation.

They mirror Inner World Discipline without being the same entity.

A good Risk Warden can stop a powerful institution from destroying itself.

---

# 3.5.19 — MARKET MAKERS

Do not turn “Market Maker” into a singular sinister villain.

Market-making actors facilitate trade, manage inventory, operate routing/port/exchange infrastructure, and remain exposed to risk.

The **Market Maker Field** is a visual nickname for a contested liquidity-rich environment, not proof that one market maker controls a range.

---

# 3.5.20 — NAMED SUPPORTING CAST PRINCIPLE

Named humans should be introduced because they create emotional contrast, philosophical conflict, new knowledge, audience access, or recurring tension.

Not because the universe “needs more characters.”

---

# 3.5.21 — ROOKIE EXPLORER

A future recurring audience-proxy role.

Purpose:
- ask natural questions,
- make basic concepts legible,
- demonstrate growth,
- let new viewers enter Market World.

Critical rule:

> **Inexperienced ≠ stupid.**

The Rookie may notice obvious human truths advanced GLX overlooks.

---

# 3.5.22 — MENTOR ARCHETYPE

GLX may encounter domain-specific mentors such as macro veterans, old Cartographers, risk specialists, or systems builders.

No single all-knowing master.

A mentor should sharpen GLX, expose blind spots, and provide history without handing him a secret final strategy.

---

# 3.5.23 — RIVAL ARCHETYPE

GLX needs rivals eventually, but rivalry should be intellectual/strategic before personal hatred.

A rival can disagree on:
- market interpretation,
- automation,
- risk,
- research philosophy,
- macro worldview,
- human vs machine balance.

The rival must be right sometimes.

Possible role types:
- Pure Quant
- Pure Discretionary
- High-Leverage Operator
- Macro Strategist
- Fast-System Builder

These are ideological mirrors, not names yet.

---

# 3.5.24 — ALLIES / STUDENTS

Allies should complement GLX rather than copy him: engineer, macro researcher, risk specialist, data scientist, historian, execution specialist.

Students should progress:

Listen → Practice → Question → Develop → Disagree → Become Independent.

Mentorship should produce independence, not permanent dependence.

---

# 3.5.25 — NON-TRADER CHARACTERS

Some recurring Outer World characters should not care much about markets.

They preserve humanity and remind GLX:

> **The Market is not the entire world.**

A Grounded Friend can interrupt obsession, challenge balance, and make Lab life look appropriately strange from outside.

---

# 3.5.26 — OUTER PRESSURE

Bills, work, social expectations, career pressure, relationships, and responsibility should be represented through normal human stakes rather than villains.

Without Outer pressure, “freedom through understanding” loses emotional consequence.

---

# 3.5.27 — CREATOR AUDIENCE AS SOCIAL FORCE

GLX's online audience can influence Ego, Fear, Greed, Curiosity, and Attention through praise, criticism, questions, virality, and expectations.

The crowd is not one character.

Some viewers are supportive, skeptical, trolling, informed, wrong, or insightful.

---

# 3.5.28 — POLICY / REAL-PERSON RULE

Named central-bank-like fantasy characters should be rare.

Policy Tower matters more than caricaturing a real public official.

Specific historical people belong primarily in documentary/historical reconstruction contexts rather than permanent fantasy cast.

---

# 3.5.29 — FICTIONAL INSTITUTION RULE

Fictional institutions should represent archetypal functions rather than secretly mapping one-to-one onto real institutions unless clearly framed.

Avoid defamatory villain analogues.

---

# 3.5.30 — CONFLICT WITHOUT CONSPIRACY

Institutional conflict comes from:
- competing mandates,
- inventory pressure,
- crowded positions,
- funding stress,
- policy change,
- liquidity need,
- information asymmetry,
- horizon conflict.

The real system is already interesting enough.

---

# 3.5.31 — INFORMATION ASYMMETRY

Different actors know different things.

A Dealer may see flow.

A macro researcher may understand policy.

A commercial hedger may know physical demand.

GLX may see cross-market structure.

CEREBUS may connect them.

No one sees everything.

---

# 3.5.32 — HORIZON CONFLICT

Two actors can take opposite positions and both be rational.

Long-horizon Asset House may buy weakness while a short-horizon Hedge House sells momentum.

Neither must be “wrong.”

Their horizons differ.

---

# 3.5.33 — CAPITAL SCALE / AGILITY

Large actors gain influence from size, but size brings slower movement, liquidity constraints, visibility, complexity, and execution problems.

Independent operators may possess:

> **the ability not to act**

A large convoy must use a highway.

GLX can sometimes take a narrow path—or wait.

---

# 3.5.34 — CONSTRAINT AS CHARACTER

Institutional constraints can become physical:
- mandate walls,
- benchmark rails,
- leverage ceilings,
- liquidity gates,
- regulatory boundaries.

This lets viewers see why large actors cannot simply do whatever they want.

---

# 3.5.35 — MORALITY / VILLAINS

An actor is not good or evil because of size.

Villainy comes from choices:
- intentional deception,
- exploitation,
- hidden risk,
- corruption,
- reckless abuse of power,
- manipulation of vulnerable people.

“Bank” is not a moral action.

Valid future antagonist archetypes include:

## False Prophet
Certainty theater, selective wins, hidden losses, absolute prediction, charisma without epistemic integrity.

## Extractor
Predatory education, scam systems, exploitative platforms, manipulation of inexperienced participants.

These are behavior-based antagonist classes, not market-size classes.

---

# 3.5.36 — SUPPORTING CAST DEPTH STANDARD

Every named recurring human should work at three levels:

## Story
Interesting person with understandable motivation.

## World Function
Adds capability, conflict, or perspective.

## Ontology
Represents a real participant type, cognitive style, or institutional role.

No disposable exposition characters.

---

# 3.5.37 — KNOWLEDGE ASYMMETRY

The cast should not share one brain.

GLX knows systems/trading/building.

Macro mentor knows policy/history.

Engineer knows infrastructure.

Rookie brings outsider questions.

Risk specialist understands capital survival.

Rival brings alternative philosophy.

Natural dialogue comes from different knowledge.

---

# 3.5.38 — CHARACTER PROGRESSION

Supporting cast can learn, change views, leave, return, surpass GLX in specific domains, make mistakes, and disagree.

Different characters should also react differently to CEREBUS: fascination, skepticism, threat, over-trust, technical curiosity, philosophical concern.

---

# 3.5.39 — CEREBUS ACCESS LAW

Potential access levels:
- Public File
- Training
- Research
- Operator
- Core

Not everyone gets full CEREBUS access.

Access itself can create story tension.

---

# 3.5.40 — GLX LAB TEAM POSSIBILITY

The Lab may eventually expand from one obsessive builder into a small multidisciplinary research crew.

This should be earned slowly.

Early loneliness matters.

---

# 3.5.41 — CAST SIZE / NAMING DELAY

Do not flood early GLX WORLD with named people.

> **Roles before names.**

Recommended first human-role pool:
1. Rookie Explorer
2. Grounded Friend
3. Macro Cartographer
4. Systems Engineer
5. Risk Warden
6. Rival Operator

Do not introduce all six immediately.

---

# 3.5.42 — STORY ARCHETYPES

Institutional stories:
- The Convoy
- The Dealer Stress
- The Policy Bell
- The Mandate Wall
- The Crowded House
- The Hedge
- The Rival Map

Human stories:
- Rookie's First Market Entry
- The Mentor Was Wrong
- The Rival Was Right
- The Student Leaves
- The Friend at the Door
- The Engineer Says No

These produce education through conflict rather than lecture.

---

# 3.5.43 — OUTER / MARKET DUAL CAST

Some humans may receive Market World representations, but these should express perspective rather than automatically granting superhero forms.

Not everyone gets a full anime transformation system.

GLX's visual transformations remain special.

---

# MASS-AUDIENCE CAST TEST

Every major supporting character must pass:

> **Would this person still be interesting if the audience knew nothing about trading?**

and:

> **Would their market role remain credible to an experienced participant?**

---

# SOCIAL WORLD NORTH STAR

> **GLX WORLD should feel populated by people and institutions pursuing different goals under shared uncertainty—not by heroes fighting a secret market-controlling cabal.**

# BLOCK 3.5 CORE LAW

> **Size creates influence. Information creates advantage. Constraints create behavior. Incentives create conflict. None of them create omnipotence.**

And:

> **Roles come before names. Characters earn permanence through story.**
