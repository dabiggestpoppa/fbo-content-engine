# GLX WORLD — PRODUCTION CONSTITUTION

## Status
Canonical production charter.

## Relationship to the Content Constitution

The Content Constitution defines **what GLX WORLD is**.

The Production Constitution defines **how GLX WORLD is turned into finished media without damaging what it is**.

When these documents conflict, the creative canon wins unless an explicit canon amendment is approved.

---

# ARTICLE I — PRODUCTION EXISTS TO SERVE THE WORLD

Production is not the source of GLX identity.

Production is the mechanism that converts:
**reality → canon → story → media → distribution → learning.**

The goal is not maximum output.

The goal is:

> **maximum durable value per idea, with quality preserved as scale increases.**

---

# ARTICLE II — GLX IS A PREMIUM IP LANE

The generic content farm may prioritize systematic scale.

GLX WORLD must prioritize:

1. canon integrity
2. character consistency
3. intellectual substance
4. original framing
5. storytelling
6. visual quality
7. emotional resonance
8. audience recognition
9. production efficiency
10. volume

Volume is last, not first.

---

# ARTICLE III — SOURCE OF TRUTH

Every GLX production should be grounded in one or more approved sources:

- real market event
- real GLX research
- CEREBUS discovery
- personal trading experience
- educational principle
- canonical GLX lore
- approved fictional story arc
- audience/community event
- intentional comedy premise

Production systems must not invent factual research claims merely to improve a story.

Fiction may dramatize reality.

It may not counterfeit evidence.

---

# ARTICLE IV — CANON GATE

Before production begins, a concept should identify:

- content pillar
- reality layer: Outer / Market / Inner / Glitch
- GLX form
- relevant characters/entities
- location
- canonical artifacts
- emotional state
- educational or narrative purpose
- lore impact
- intended audience depth

If a concept requires breaking canon, it must be marked as:
**NON-CANON**, **DREAM**, **PARODY**, **WHAT-IF**, or approved as a canon amendment.

---

# ARTICLE V — ONE IDEA, MANY EXPRESSIONS

A strong source idea may become:

- Chibi reaction
- meme
- caricature poster
- anime hero frame
- cinematic key art
- 15-second short
- 30–60 second episode
- carousel
- educational breakdown
- CEREBUS File
- long-form video
- Discord/community piece
- sticker/reaction
- follow-up lore scene

These are adaptations of one idea, not duplicate spam.

Every adaptation should earn its form.

---

# ARTICLE VI — STYLE ASSIGNMENT

### Caricature
Use for reality, personality, Lab work, research, behind-the-scenes, personal reflection.

### Anime
Use for movement, conflict, market metaphors, battles, high-energy explanations.

### Chibi
Use for inner emotion, punchlines, reactions, psychology, stickers, fast social communication.

### Cinematic Glitch
Use for rare high-impact moments, revelations, launches, milestones, mysteries, season-level events.

Cinematic treatment should remain scarce.

---

# ARTICLE VII — CHARACTER CONSISTENCY GATE

Every production containing GLX should preserve:
- hot-pink fuzzy hat
- black shades unless canonically removed
- facial identity
- facial hair
- warm medium-dark skin tone
- gold chain where appropriate
- black-dominant wardrobe
- recognizable silhouette
- form-specific rules

Identity outranks stylistic purity.

---

# ARTICLE VIII — STORY BEFORE ASSET

Do not begin with:
> “Make me an image.”

Begin with:
> “What is happening?”

Every meaningful production should know:
- setup
- viewpoint
- tension
- change
- payoff

Even a still image should imply a moment before and a moment after.

---

# ARTICLE IX — ANIMATION-READY THINKING

Key art should be designed with possible motion in mind.

Where practical, think in layers:
- foreground character
- character accessories
- midground props
- background environment
- particles/weather
- screens/data
- typography
- lighting/glitch elements

This allows still art to become:
- parallax
- camera pushes
- screen animation
- eye/glasses effects
- particle movement
- lip-sync or voice sequences
- short cinematic scenes

---

# ARTICLE X — VOICE AND AUDIO

GLX voice must follow the Voice Bible.

Core dialogue architecture:
- **CEREBUS = Logic**
- **GLX = Judgment**
- **Chibi = Emotion**

Audio should support state:
- Quant = scanning/data language
- Warrior = kinetic impact
- Macro = scale/spatial atmosphere
- Glitch = distortion
- Broken = quiet/muffled
- Ascendant = minimal clarity

Voice cloning or synthetic voice is a production method, not permission to alter character voice.

---

# ARTICLE XI — PLATFORM ADAPTATION

The story is canonical.

The packaging is flexible.

A platform may change:
- duration
- crop
- hook timing
- subtitle density
- caption length
- thumbnail
- CTA
- audio mix

A platform should not change:
- meaning
- factual claim
- character identity
- lore status
- core visual signature

---

# ARTICLE XII — ORIGINALITY LAW

Generic farm content may remix public trends.

GLX WORLD should primarily transform original or legitimately sourced material into original GLX expression.

Trend participation is allowed.

Trend dependence is not.

A trend should pass through the GLX lens until the result feels native to GLX WORLD.

---

# ARTICLE XIII — REUSE WITHOUT DILUTION

Reusable elements are encouraged:
- characters
- locations
- expressions
- poses
- artifacts
- transitions
- sound motifs
- UI elements
- scene templates

But repetition should build recognition, not create laziness.

Reuse the language of the world.

Do not endlessly duplicate finished ideas.

---

# ARTICLE XIV — QUALITY GATES

A GLX piece should be rejected or revised if it:
- looks like generic AI art
- breaks character identity
- uses cinematic treatment without narrative weight
- has fake or overstated market claims
- uses guru language
- overloads logos
- contains random meaningless tech
- uses lore with no purpose
- is visually strong but intellectually empty
- is educational but aesthetically interchangeable with any trading page
- cannot explain why its chosen form/style was used

---

# ARTICLE XV — PRODUCTION CLASSES

### Class A — Signal
Fast reaction content.
Chibi, meme, simple commentary.

### Class B — Standard Episode
Caricature/anime scene, short-form story, educational content.

### Class C — Feature
Multi-scene short, deep carousel, major CEREBUS File, strong narrative.

### Class D — Event
Cinematic launch, season reveal, major research breakthrough, milestone.

Production effort should match class.

---

# ARTICLE XVI — CONTENT MEMORY

Every shipped GLX piece should ideally retain:
- source idea
- canon references
- characters/forms used
- location
- content class
- platforms
- publish date
- performance
- audience response
- reusable assets
- follow-up opportunities
- lore consequences

The archive should become smarter over time.

---

# ARTICLE XVII — ANALYTICS SERVES LEARNING

Analytics may answer:
- what hooks retain attention
- what characters resonate
- what formats convert
- what series deserve expansion
- what jokes recur well
- what educational depth performs
- which visual styles fit which ideas

Analytics must not automatically erase creative identity in pursuit of short-term metrics.

A viral generic post that weakens GLX may be less valuable than a smaller post that strengthens recognition.

---

# ARTICLE XVIII — HUMAN CREATIVE AUTHORITY

Automated systems may:
- collect source material
- propose ideas
- adapt formats
- generate drafts
- render assets
- schedule
- publish
- localize
- track performance

They should not independently:
- redefine canon
- create permanent character changes
- reveal major mysteries
- retcon lore
- make unsupported research claims
- permanently change voice
- declare a new season arc

Those require explicit creative approval.

---

# ARTICLE XIX — THE PRODUCTION NORTH STAR

> **The audience should experience an expanding universe, not a content factory.**

The machinery should disappear behind the finished work.

The better the production system becomes, the less the audience should notice the system.

They should notice:
- GLX
- the story
- the insight
- the joke
- the world
- the feeling

---

# ARTICLE XX — FINAL PRODUCTION TEST

Before publishing:

> **Does this feel like an episode from GLX WORLD, or an asset generated by a pipeline?**

If it feels like the pipeline, production is not finished.
